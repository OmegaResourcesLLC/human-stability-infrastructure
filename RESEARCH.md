# Research Status

Human Stability Infrastructure is an exploratory research and design project.

The repository brings together several related concepts that have been developed through working papers, lived experience, professional experience, systems thinking, and continuing investigation into how instability develops across connected domains.

The major concepts represented here are:

- Human Standability
- Stability Intelligence
- Organic Stability Intelligence
- Stability Infrastructure
- CORA, or Coordinated Outcome & Recovery Architecture

These concepts are related, but they are not interchangeable.

Human Standability concerns functional continuity under pressure at the level of a person.

Stability Intelligence concerns the broader research question of how meaningful changes in stability might be observed, understood, and reviewed without collapsing a person's life into a single score.

Organic Stability Intelligence examines stability at the level of interconnected systems, environments, organizations, communities, and resource relationships.

Stability Infrastructure describes the connective and maintenance layer that may help otherwise fragmented systems preserve continuity.

CORA is a separate exploratory architecture that may eventually provide an operational environment for some forms of human-reviewed coordination. CORA is not proof of the other frameworks and should not be treated as the definition of Stability Infrastructure.

## Current evidence status

Version 0.1 should be understood as a research artifact and implementation discussion framework.

The repository does not currently establish:

- validated causal relationships;
- validated stability measures;
- predictive accuracy;
- universal thresholds;
- universal intervention sequences;
- clinical effectiveness;
- legal validity;
- eligibility criteria;
- sentencing or supervision criteria; or
- evidence that using this repository improves participant outcomes.

Where the repository proposes relationships between conditions, those relationships should be treated as questions to investigate unless supported by external evidence appropriate to the context.

## Relationship to existing fields

This work does not claim to replace existing research or practice involving case management, integrated care, recovery capital, social determinants of health, prevention science, trauma-informed practice, systems of care, public health, housing stability, reentry, behavioral health, workforce development, family support, or other established fields.

Where Human Stability Infrastructure overlaps with existing work, that overlap should be identified rather than hidden.

A useful framework should make existing knowledge easier to connect and apply. It does not become more valuable by pretending prior work does not exist.
