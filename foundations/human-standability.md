# Human Standability

Human Standability is an exploratory concept concerned with functional continuity under pressure.

The basic question is not whether a person appears stable from the outside. It is whether the person can continue functioning when ordinary pressures, disruptions, responsibilities, and unexpected events begin interacting.

A person may look stationary while using enormous effort simply to prevent further loss.

Someone may be keeping a job while losing reliable transportation. Another person may be attending required appointments while housing becomes unstable. A parent may appear to be meeting every obligation while childcare, food, communication, and income pressures are quietly consuming the capacity that would otherwise allow forward movement.

Human Standability asks us to notice that difference.

It does not assume that continuity can be represented accurately by one score, and it does not give an institution ownership over the meaning of stability in another person's life.

Any future attempt to measure aspects of Human Standability should remain contextual, revisable, participant-centered, and open to disagreement.

The concept is currently exploratory and requires validation.
