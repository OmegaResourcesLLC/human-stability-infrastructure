# Synthetic Worked Example

## Purpose

This example shows how several reasoning roles might examine the same situation without producing a score or pretending there is one correct intervention sequence.

Everything below is fictional.

No real participant information should be inferred from this example.

## Scenario

Jordan is a fictional parent who recently moved from temporary housing into an apartment.

Jordan has started a new job and is trying to maintain regular work hours.

The bus route used to reach work requires a transfer. A recent schedule change has made the connection unreliable during the early morning shift.

Jordan has already arrived late twice.

Childcare is available, but the provider closes before Jordan can reliably return on the evening route.

Jordan's phone was disconnected after an unexpected expense, which has made it harder for the employer, childcare provider, and housing worker to reach Jordan.

Jordan also needs to replace an identification document required for an upcoming employment-related process.

None of these conditions alone necessarily represents a crisis.

Together, they are beginning to place pressure on several parts of Jordan's life.

## Coordinator review

The coordination problem appears to involve timing rather than a simple absence of services.

Jordan currently has housing, employment, childcare, and public transportation available, but the schedules do not reliably fit together.

The disconnected phone is making coordination harder because several people depend on it to communicate changes.

Questions worth following up include whether the employer has any temporary scheduling flexibility, whether another transportation option exists for the specific shift transition, whether the childcare arrangement can be adjusted, and which issue Jordan considers most urgent.

No conclusion should be made before asking Jordan.

## Participant Perspective Review

The current description tells us what several systems require, but it does not yet tell us what Jordan believes is most likely to fail first.

Jordan may consider the phone the central problem because restoring communication could make several other arrangements easier.

Jordan may instead believe transportation is the immediate threat to employment.

The next step should include Jordan's own explanation of the tradeoffs rather than assuming that professionals can identify the priority from the outside.

## Evidence Review

```
SOURCE: Case narrative provided for this scenario.
EVIDENCE: Bus schedule change; two documented late arrivals; childcare closing time; phone disconnection; identification document requirement.
OBSERVATION: Several time-sensitive commitments currently overlap in ways that appear difficult to meet simultaneously.
INTERPRETATION: The overlapping commitments may be placing cumulative pressure on Jordan's ability to maintain employment and childcare arrangements.
ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATION: The two late arrivals could reflect a temporary adjustment period rather than an ongoing pattern; more instances, or their absence, would clarify this.
MISSING INFORMATION: Whether the employer has flexibility, what alternative transportation exists, and what Jordan considers the priority.
UNCERTAINTY: Moderate. The documented conditions are consistent with rising pressure, but the scenario does not establish that any specific outcome (job loss, housing loss) is imminent.
QUESTION FOR HUMAN REVIEW: Before proposing a response, ask Jordan which of these pressures Jordan considers most urgent to resolve first.
```

Any report stating that Jordan is at imminent risk of losing employment would be stronger than the information provided.

## Safeguard Review

A coordination tool should not convert these conditions into a hidden risk score that follows Jordan into unrelated systems.

Only information necessary for the coordination purpose should be used.

Jordan should be able to correct factual errors and disagree with the interpretation.

Difficulty meeting several schedules should not automatically become evidence of unreliability, instability, or noncompliance.

## Implementation Stress Test

A plan requiring three agencies to participate in a new weekly meeting is unlikely to be the first thing worth testing.

A smaller test would be to determine whether one person can help Jordan map the timing conflict, identify which organizations actually have flexibility, and confirm whether one change reduces pressure elsewhere.

If the organization cannot accomplish that basic coordination step with existing capacity, the framework has identified an implementation constraint rather than solved it.

## What this example demonstrates

The roles do not produce five separate plans for Jordan.

They help a human reviewer see different parts of the same situation.

The Coordinator examines relationships between moving pieces.

The Participant Perspective Reviewer identifies where Jordan's voice is missing.

The Evidence Reviewer prevents concern from quietly becoming certainty.

The Safeguard Reviewer examines how the information could be misused.

The Implementation Stress Tester asks whether the proposed response could actually happen.

The useful output is a clearer conversation that helps people understand the situation without turning Jordan into a score.
