# Participant Voice

A system can gather extensive information about a person and still misunderstand what is happening.

Participant voice is therefore not an optional courtesy.

The person living through the situation may see dependencies, tradeoffs, risks, strengths, and priorities that are invisible from an institutional record.

That does not mean every interpretation must automatically be accepted as fact. It means disagreement should be visible rather than silently overwritten.

Where a participant and a professional understand the situation differently, the difference itself may be important information.

Human Stability Infrastructure should preserve room for the person to explain what they believe is happening, what matters most to them, what support they consider useful, and where they believe the system has misunderstood them.
