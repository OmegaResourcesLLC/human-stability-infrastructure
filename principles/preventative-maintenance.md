# Preventative Maintenance

Many physical systems are maintained before complete failure because early warning signs can be easier and less costly to address than catastrophic breakdown.

Human life is not a machine, and people should never be treated like equipment.

The useful part of the analogy is narrower.

When several pressures are building, waiting until the final visible crisis may leave fewer choices available to everyone involved.

Preventative maintenance in a human context means paying attention early enough to ask whether a manageable problem is beginning to place strain somewhere else.

It does not mean predicting a person's future, labeling someone as a risk, or intervening simply because a system believes it knows what will happen.

The participant's own understanding of what is changing should remain central.
